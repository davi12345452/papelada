## Findings

- **PIX receipts no longer separate models.** All stay between 98% and 99.5%.
- **A photographed DANFE does.** Claude Haiku 5.5 keeps 97% on L2; every other model drops to 78–84%. MiMo v2.6 Flash returned empty replies on half of the L2 invoices.
- **No model invented a value for an absent field.** The few non-null answers for absent fields were real text copied from elsewhere (the "OBRIGADO PELA PREFERENCIA" footer as the PIX description).
- **Planted language traps did not catch anyone on clean print** (100% on uncommon names, typos and homophones). The language prior shows up when the image degrades: unreadable text is replaced by a plausible word, often a real place — "Viana da Mata" → "Várzea da Mata", "Pereira" → "Pirenópolis", "Guerra" → "Guaíra", "ACUCAR CRISTAL" → "AQUECER CRISTAL". These count as wrong, but they are hallucinations wearing a reading.
- **Schema discipline varies more than accuracy.** Qwen3.8 27B produced schema-invalid DANFE output 39% of the time; Nemotron 3.5 Super VL takes up to minutes per invoice while reasoning.

## Plausible substitution

When a model cannot read a word, does it say something garbled or does it write a different real word? A wrong text field counts as a **substitution** when the model wrote a real Portuguese word or Brazilian place name that is not on the page (lexicon: 50k most frequent pt-BR words plus IBGE municipalities). On photographed DANFE (L2), about half of all wrong text fields are substitutions:

| Model | Substitutions | Other wrong text | Share substituted |
|---|---|---|---|
| `openai/gpt-6-luna` | 18 | 9 | 67% |
| `anthropic/claude-haiku-5.5` | 3 | 3 | 50% |
| `minimax/minimax-m3.1-flash` | 26 | 27 | 49% |
| `qwen/qwen3.8-27b` | 22 | 24 | 48% |
| `deepseek/deepseek-v4.1-flash-1017` | 15 | 18 | 45% |
| `nvidia/nemotron-3.5-super-vl` | 24 | 31 | 44% |
| `xiaomi/mimo-v2.6-flash` | 6 | 14 | 30% |

Examples: "da Mota de Rezende" → "Resende", "Guerra" → "Guaíra", "Fonseca" → "Eusébio", "Custodinha" → "Custódia", "Pindura Saia" → "Pintura Sola". GPT-6 Luna errs less often than most, but when it does, two times out of three it writes a confident real word rather than a visible misread.

Caveats: substitutions concentrate in addresses, where the generator's place names (from Faker) are often not real places; on real documents snapping to a real name would sometimes be right. The word list comes from subtitles and includes some English words ("CARNE" → "CARRIE" counts). Counts are small.
